Project Goal

The need for this project arose from a quick realization that we needed to stay centered in an apple trellis to pick effectively and reduce downtime. Being unable to stay centered introduces the following issues:

  • The work volume of our robots became limited or out of range.
  • Robots are more likely to snag when driving towards one side of the trellis.
  • The harvester is unable to correct its steering angle, and you risk damaging the orchard.

The reason for this was mainly that our motors did not follow the commanded speed accurately enough to keep us centered in the row.

Therefore the overall goal of this project was to enhance the drive system software for our robotic apple harvester in order to provide precise motor control in rough terrain

High Level Overview

Before I jump into some of the key challenges and pivotal decisions, let me give you some context about the system I had to become familiar with.

  • Control Systems: PID Control

Key Challenge 1: Integrating Hardware Components:

Wiring and Components

A challenge I encountered early on was having to understand all these individual components enough to do the wiring on them:

  • 48-volt power supply - 4 batteries in series
  • 48-volt Contactor - Driven by analog out on the Sevcon
  • 24-volt Relay - Driven by enable signal. Supplies key switch signal to Sevcon
  • I&W Drive - Interfaces with Sevcon through UVW phase leads and Amphenol connector
  • Sevcon Gen4 Controller - Controls I&W drive
  • 32-pin Ampseal connector - Consolidates wiring from all components and plugs into Sevcon
  • 8-pin Amphenol connector - Carries motor encoder, temperature, and brake signals.

Sin Cos Encoder Installation

  • I needed to configure the Sevcon with the min and max encoder sin cos voltages
    • Probed the encoder signals with a scope and manually rotated the motor to find the peak and trough voltages
  • Encountered a shorted brake wire and had to do a teardown of the motor to fix it
    • Had to remove the rotor and stator to access the brake wire
  • Had to become familiar with PMAC motors

Pivotal Decision 1: Speed VS Torque Mode

  • Sevcon had two modes to choose from: torque and speed mode
  • Pros of selecting speed mode:
    • Provides internal PI velocity controller
    • Control loop can run at high frequencies - would have been otherwise limited by rate at which measured velocity was published
    • Provides limited low speed gain scheduling
    • Less prone to error
  • Cons of selecting speed mode:
    • Unable to combat integrator windup
    • Unable to implement feed forward
    • Unable to implement more complex gain scheduling
    • Manual driving is less intuitive

Key Challenge 2: Testing Setup and Iteration Time

  • We had unit tests but no HWITL testing

Key Challenge 3: Inadequate Control at Low Speeds

  • The harvester length was twice as wide as the wheel spacing was wide
    • This acted as a lever that doubled our error at the nose of the harvester
  • The Sevcon did not implement a signal limit on the compensation
    • This allowed for integrator windup
  • Gains needed to be robust enough to support both high and low speeds
  • Tuning gains at the sevcon controller level was not sufficient to reach desired positon

Pivotal Decision 3: Custom PID Position Controller

  • Sought to further improve low speed control by wrapping a position controller around the Sevcon's PI loop
  • Allowed for unique feed forward implementations
  • Implemented signal limits
  • Use encoder for position measurement

Position Feedback from Sin Cos Encoders

  • Decided to wire sin and cos signals to analog inputs on the Sevcon
  • Had to implement class to calculate the mechanical angle of the motor from incoming sin and cos values
  • Could not implement during manual control due to nyquist check

Key Challenge 4: Issues with Manual Driving

  • Joystick was linearly mapped
  • Braking during manual drive was not responsive

Pivotal Decision 5: Exponential Joystick Map

Implemented an exponential joystick map to provide better handling at low to mid speeds while still providing full power at full deflection.

Pivotal Decision 6: Better Braking Behavior

    In this project I also chose to refactor our Sevcon controller state machine as there were complaints of the drives continuing to move after commanding zero speed.

  • Chose to go into state braked in two different ways
    • Slow to a stop and then engage brakes
    • Immediately engage brakes
  • Improved safety by preventing the drives from moving after being commanded zero speed

Lessons Learned

What I would do differently knowing what I know now:

  • Try to use more open source CANopen libraries for commissioning of Sevcons
    • One example I can think of is the canopen module which has a dcf programmer and reader you can use to quickly configure CANOpen devices.
  • Spend less time on parts of the project that are less likely to have a high ROI
    • For example, I spent a lot of time trying to tune the Sevcon velocity gains to be perfect when I quickly tuned them to a point where they were good enough.
  • Reach out to the companies whose components you are using. They are the most knowledgeable about their product and may be able to answer a question in minutes.
  • Invest the time in producing robust and scalable analysis tools
